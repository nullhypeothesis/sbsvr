Support

Help with Side-by-Side VR for AR Glasses. Still stuck? Email us.

What you need

  • A Mac with macOS 13 Ventura or later.
  • XREAL One or One Pro glasses, connected to the Mac with a USB-C cable.

Getting started

  1. Connect the glasses. In System Settings → Displays, set them to Extended display, not mirroring.
  2. Open a video: drag it onto the player, use File → Open…, use Open With → Side-by-Side VR in Finder, or paste a link with File → Open Link….
  3. Click Play on glasses. The app switches the glasses to 3D and plays the video full screen on them, and switches them back to 2D when you’re done.

The app recognises 180°, 360°, fisheye and flat videos, side by side or top and bottom, from their shape and name. If it guesses wrong, change it under Video format.

No glasses to hand? Help → Play Test Pattern shows a test video; drag the preview to look around.

Playing videos from Safari

  1. In Safari, open Settings → Extensions and turn on Side-by-Side VR.
  2. Allow it on the websites you watch from (or on every website).
  3. Hover over a video and click Play on Glasses, or use the extension’s toolbar button.

The video continues on the glasses from the same spot, and it works on sites you’re signed in to.

In the glasses

Move the mouse to show the controls. The sliders button opens more settings: projection, stereo layout, swap eyes, eye alignment, volume, controls depth, motion prediction and calibration.

  • Center view (T or double-click) brings whatever you’re looking at to straight ahead at eye level, so you don’t have to crane your neck. Level (Shift T) undoes it.
  • Head tracking (M) keeps the video fixed in the room as you turn your head. Off (the default), the glasses’ own Follow / Anchor modes work as usual.
  • Drag with the mouse to move the picture; scroll to zoom.

Keyboard

Space play/pause · ← → seek 10 s (Shift 60 s) · ↑ ↓ volume · R recenter · T center view · F play on glasses / stop · P projection · S stereo layout · X swap eyes · - = zoom · M head tracking · C calibrate · L loop · H help

Troubleshooting

“Glasses display not found”

Check that the glasses show up in System Settings → Displays as an extended display. Try unplugging the cable and plugging it back in.

The picture looks doubled, or 3D strains my eyes

Some videos are filmed or encoded with the eyes out of line. Open the sliders button and adjust Eye spacing and Eye height until the two pictures merge; this is remembered for that video. If near things look far away and far things near, try Swap eyes (X).

The video looks stretched or warped

The format was probably guessed wrong. Press P to step through the projections (180°, 360°, fisheye lenses, flat) and S for the stereo layout.

The glasses’ X button doesn’t switch Follow / Anchor

While head tracking is on, the glasses hand their motion sensor to the app and their X button only recenters. Turn head tracking off (M) to use the glasses’ own modes again.

With head tracking, the view drifts or wobbles

Press R to recenter (a slow sideways drift is normal). Run Calibrate once with the glasses on, and if motion lags or overshoots, adjust Motion prediction.

A web video won’t play

  • For sites you sign in to, or whose player builds the video inside the page, play it from Safari with Play on Glasses instead of pasting the link.
  • “The site refused…” or “error 403”: the site only lets its own player load the video.
  • “The link may have expired”: start the video on the site again and click Play on Glasses.
  • “Can’t be decoded”: the video uses a format your Mac can’t play.

The Play on Glasses button doesn’t appear in Safari

Check that the extension is turned on in Safari → Settings → Extensions and allowed on that website in Safari → Settings → Websites. Reload the page afterwards.
